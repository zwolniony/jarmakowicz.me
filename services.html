Ways to work together

Talks and workshops

AI at work

A talk or hands-on session for leadership teams, conferences and sector organisations. We look at what really changes when AI joins people's work, whose judgement it touches, and how to introduce it without eroding the know-how you depend on.

You get: a 30 to 60 minute talk, or a half-day workshop, shaped around your sector.
For: management teams, events, professional networks

Half-day to two days

Orientation sprint

Together we map where your time goes, which tasks an agent could take on, and what should stay with people. You leave with a short, concrete plan and an honest view of the risks around data, cost and dependence on vendors.

You get: a short written plan with the first workflows to try and the main risks to manage.
For: organisations deciding whether and where to start

Two to four weeks

Personal research agent

An agent set up for one professional: it tracks new publications in your field, keeps your notes and sources in order, and drafts from your own material. Karim at Wheels of Impact, for example, uses one to bring new academic research on diversity, equity and inclusion into his consulting.

You get: a working agent connected to your sources, and a short guide to running it.
For: researchers, consultants, analysts, writers

Around three months

Team assistant

A shared assistant for a whole team, running on a server you own and working inside your existing tools. We build the first workflows together, and by the end someone on your team runs it without me.

You get: a working assistant, three to five workflows in daily use, documentation and one trained team member.
For: NGOs, research groups, consultancies, policy organisations

How I build agents

I work with Hermes, an open-source agent by Nous Research, and connect it to the tools you already use, such as Notion, Airtable, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. It reads and updates them directly, so your team keeps working where it already works, without a parallel system that someone has to keep up to date.

People talk to it the way they'd message a colleague. Over time it saves reusable instructions for the jobs that keep coming back, which you can read and edit, so it gets more useful the longer it runs. Access starts read-only, and we add write access tool by tool once you trust it.

What people use it for

Answering "how did we do this last time?" with links to the original notes. Spotting when two colleagues are building the same thing. Keeping up with new research and policy in a field. Turning meeting notes into first drafts in your own templates. Moving content safely when a team changes tools.

We start where you lose the most time, and add more once the first workflow earns its place.

Your data stays yours

Agents I build run on infrastructure you own, hosted in the EU if you prefer. You decide what they can see and what they keep. The AI model underneath can be swapped whenever you like, whether that's Claude, GPT or an open-source model, which matters if you handle sensitive material or need to stay neutral.

I sign a data processing agreement with every client, in line with the GDPR, and set these limits up with the care that sensitive material needs.
